My programming experience

Thursday, 03/09/2026

High school

The first time I learned programming was in computer science class in high school. I had a lot of fun learning the basics of Python and SQL, and even some C programming with an Arduino. The year after we had to make a game in JavaScript, for which I made a game consisting of small tasks or minigames, that keeps going faster and faster and the goal is to keep up as long as possible. You can play and view the terrible code here.

In that same year me and Simon Kappetijn (who is also in this class) made our own rhythm game for our graduation project (profielwerkstuk). It was quite a simple game, press keys to catch the falling arrows at the right time. There was a twist though, there were multiple groups of participants who received feedback in different ways. We then collected the data and found out whether positive feedback actually improves your performance compared to more negative feedback. To do that we learned how to use Godot Engine, which was honestly tough but it turned out pretty alright. You can watch or skip through the video below if you'd like to see how it turned out.

After high school

After high school I worked on one more project, called VALORANT Locker. You see, me and my friends enjoy playing VALORANT, a 5v5 tactical shooter in which you all have to pick an agent (a character) every game. As a team you want to pick a solid combination of agents, however the best combination can change every game, dependant on what map we are playing, what combination of 5 friends are currently playing, how good each player is in each role or agent, and more. Thus, we thought it would be fun to build a Discord bot that would tell everyone which agent to pick, when we tell it who is playing and what map we are playing on. We ended up designing a points system that would give every agent a rating for each player, and the ratings would change based on the map, and it would update after every game. I coded in Python this together with a friend. Now that we had the points, we actually had to make a solid combinations of characters using those points. You cant just simply put the highest rated agent for every player, since there might be duplicates and they might not work well together. So we made a couple of rules that every composition had to abide by. Then I coded a backtracking algorithm to quickly find the best composition points-wise, while still abiding by every rule. I won't go into how it works here because it's complicated, but if you are interested in how such an algorithm works, you can search online for "backtracking algorithm".

This covers all of the most important projects I've worked on. Thanks for reading.
