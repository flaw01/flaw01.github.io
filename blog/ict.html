What does a back-end developer actually do?

Monday, 26/10/2026

What is back-end development?

When you visit a website, or use an application, most of what you see and interact with is the front-end. The back-end is where everything behind the scenes happens. Back-end developers work on the systems that make applications function. For example, when you log into a website, the back-end will check whether your credentials are correct.

What does the job look like?

Back-end developers spend their time designing systems, writing code, testing their code, fixing bugs and maintaining existing applications. They often work together with front-end developers or other team members to make sure everthing works properly.

Code quality is very important, as applications need to be reliable, secure and efficient. Back-end developers have to think what kind of problems could arise, and find ways to solve them. For example, an application might work well with 10 users, but would it still work as well with thousands of users at the same time?

Why does this interest me?

What I find particularly interesting about back-end development is the problem-solving aspect. I enjoy figuring out how things should work and then actually implementing that logic in code. I experienced something similar while working on my VALORANT Locker project, where I had to develop an algorithm that could find the best combination of characters while following certain rules.

I also like the idea of creating systems that people actually rely on, even if they don't directly see the work behind them. I think back-end development would allow me to combine my interest in programming, algorithms and problem-solving while working on meaningful projects.
